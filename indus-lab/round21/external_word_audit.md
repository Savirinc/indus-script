# External elephant/ivory word audit

11 October 2026. Comparative reconstruction and proposed Meluhhan attribution are different claims.

## Source-backed comparative anchor

Proto-Dravidian *pal 'tooth' is a published reconstruction, independently checked in Masato Kobayashi (2004), Historical Phonology of Old Indo-Aryan Consonants, printed p163/PDF173. It is not an attested Meluhhan word. Source: https://gengo.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/masatok/hpiac_2004.pdf

## Borrowing proposal under review

Mukhopadhyay (2021) proposes a connection between the Dravidian tooth family and elephant/ivory terminology in western Asia. Proposed transformations *pal to *pīl/*piḷ and pīlu, their dating, their assignment to Indus speakers, and the borrowing route require separate evidence. Source: https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-021-00868-w

The article cites Akkadian pīru(m) 'elephant' in an Old Babylonian wisdom text through Lambert (1960), pp272–273, and CAD P, pp418–420. The dictionary lemma was checked by the source-review agent at https://dnms.org/dal/p#P0588 ; Lambert's tablet edition and dating were not independently verified in this checkpoint.

Speiser's Introduction to Hurrian, printed p46, gives ši-(in-)ni-be-e-ru-uḫ-ḫe 'ivory', referring to the Mitanni letter II59 and III97. Speiser explicitly identifies it as an Akkadian loan with a Hurrian adjectival suffix. This passage was independently checked by both the review agent and the main agent. It is not independent evidence that Meluhhan speakers used a matching word. The 2021 article's abbreviated transliteration should not replace the fuller source form. Source: https://theswissbay.ch/pdf/Books/Linguistics/Mega%20linguistics%20pack/Cuneiform/Hurrian,%20Introduction%20to%20(Speiser).pdf

The review agent also checked the later Old Persian DSf inscription, lines43–45, referring to ivory from Kush, India and Arachosia. Commodity provenance does not establish a word's donor language. Transcription/translation: https://www.livius.org/sources/content/achaemenid-royal-inscriptions/dsf/

## Decision

Retain *pal 'tooth' as a published comparative reconstruction. Keep any proposed Meluhhan elephant form in a hypothesis ledger, with no assigned Indus sign. This audit neither proves nor refutes the entire borrowing proposal. A usable decipherment constraint requires independently supported sound correspondences, chronology, donor attribution and a consistent connection to inscriptions.
