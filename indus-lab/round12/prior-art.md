## What is already established as research territory

**Rao2018, The Indus Script and Economics**, pp518–525, studies22 three-sided miniature tablets, proposes ration-token and administrative functions, and compares patterned inscriptions with proto-Elamite/proto-cuneiform practice. His economic identifications are hypotheses. Whole-object comparison and stroke-plus-sign fields are therefore prior art. Our H-315/316/317 comparison is an auditable replication and data-model improvement, not discovery of numerical notation.
https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Indus-Script.pdf (turn90view0; relevant printed518–525).

**Fuls2020, Ancient Writing and Modern Technologies—Structural Analysis of Numerical Indus Inscriptions**, explicitly considers long/short/stacked strokes, replacements by candidate nonstroke numerical signs, and sign700's restricted stroke distribution. The chapter acknowledges uncertainty in value, positional versus additive notation, and internal ordering. Proposed values and volumetric conversions are author interpretations, not established facts. A new frequency plot alone adds little; disagreement between rival frozen encodings on independently observed objects would add more.
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361812318_Ancient_Writing_and_Modern_Technologies_-_Structural_Analysis_of_Numerical_Indus_Inscriptions (turn90view2/turn91view0; especially printed66–70).

**Yadav and Vahia2011, Indus Script: A Study of its Sign Design**, already decomposes signs into basic signs, provisional bases and modifiers, and tests sign environments and positional changes. TheirTables8a/8b compare one modifier across two bases. Thus component detection is not itself novel. Our possible contribution is photograph-validated, source-versioned replication with out-of-family prediction and controls against carving style.
https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Indus-sign-design.pdf (turn90view1/turn93view3; Tables8a/8b and summary).

**Bonta2010, The Indus Valley Script: A New Interpretation**, is directly cited by Rao for patterned texts and metrological proposals. Author-hosted version located, but this review did not independently establish all its derivations or proposed values. Treat as hypothesis-generating primary interpretation, not accepted ground truth.
https://www.academia.edu/8691466/The_Indus_Valley_Script_A_New_Interpretation (turn93view0).

**Kenoyer2010, Measuring the Harappan World**, distinguishes actual material measurement from assumptions of universal rigid standardization. It documents regional/chronological variation and emphasizes more precise context. The appropriate external anchor is a measured relevant object, not an inscription's carrier dimensions presumed to encode its contents.
https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer%202010%20Measuring%20the%20Harappan%20World.pdf (turn92view0/turn94view1; printed110 and120–121).

## Five falsifiable questions

All tests below are proposals; novelty is unknown until a fuller literature review. All current corpus records have already been explored: resplitting them is internal validation, not untouched archaeological confirmation.

### 1. Does linking faces explain more than accidental pairing?

**Prediction:** observed associations between an entire non-stroke face and a stroke-plus700 face exceed associations from re-pairing faces within the same site, subtype, material and available phase.
**Execution:** include every eligible multi-face object, including families with no variation; lock aliases and mould groups; use a fixed conditional association statistic and stratified permutation. Report sparse strata rather than combine them opportunistically. Predeclare missing-data handling.
**Failure:** little excess over the restricted null, or excess vanishes when one large copied family is removed.
**Value:** tests whether an object-level relationship survives preservation/manufacture confounds. Does not distinguish quantity from rank, category or ritual grade. Prior art:Rao whole-object interpretation; exact test novelty unknown.

### 2. Are the long and short stroke series interchangeable in matching contexts?

**Prediction under interchangeability:** after replacing each candidate with its independently counted strokes, long-versus-short form cannot be reliably predicted from adjacent signs/opposite-face patterns within matched site/type/phase contexts.
**Execution:** photograph-confirm small candidate set; preserve short-linear versus stacked forms; compare exact contexts and held-out mould-family prediction. A low-powered null does not prove equivalence; report equivalence bounds only if sample supports them.
**Failure of interchangeability:** persistent context-dependent form selection, or stable opposite-face contrast. That would require separate signs/units/roles in later models, without identifying which.
**Prior art:** Fuls/Wells explicitly distinguish classes; claim no novel discovery of this distinction.

### 3. Does variable-field structure generalize beyond the already selected27 families?

**Prediction:** a rule learned from a fixed set of complete object families predicts which positions vary and which remain fixed in separately audited families better than a site/subtype/face-length baseline.
**Execution:** freeze family definitions before inspecting new photos; hold entire exact-text and mould families together; assess all tested families, including failures. Newly photographed old records improve transcription verification but are not new text discoveries.
**Failure:** performance depends on shared full formulae or one large family, or no gain beyond baseline.
**Prior art:** patterned-text grammar is established; leakage-resistant prospective photo audit is a methodological extension, novelty unknown.

### 4. Does a repeated graphic modifier have a consistent effect across different bases?

**Prediction:** a modifier-associated positional/context shift estimated on several bases predicts its effect on held-out bases and photo-confirmed variants.
**Execution:** map Yadav/Vahia's published modifier inventory to our namespace with reviewed concordance; compare sign position and neighbours; match frequency/length/site/type and inspect engraving differences. Never infer components solely from disconnected font pixels.
**Failure:** effects reverse across bases or disappear after controls; then a universal modifier rule is weakened.
**Prior art:**Yadav/Vahia's Tables8a/8b already demonstrate selected analogous shifts. Broader controlled generalization remains the question.

### 5. Does the restricted stroke inventory survive chronology and workshop controls?

**Prediction:** sign700's restricted values and counterpart-face associations recur within multiple archaeological contexts, not merely pooled across manufacture types and periods.
**Execution:** use literal time/period/phase fields only after catalogue verification; compare separateTAB:B/TAB:I, material and mound strata, then leave-one-context-out sensitivity. Establish minimum sample/metadata coverage before modelling; if unavailable, output the exact missing object context queue.
**Failure:** the pattern is confined to a single context or collapses after removing copied mould sets. This would favour local convention over a civilization-wide rule, without deciding its meaning.
**Prior art:** Wells already describes mound/type differences; our added question is robustness to aliases, copying and sparse metadata.

## Leadership recommendation

Prioritize independently measured inscribed vessels and robust whole-object identity over another broad unsupervised clustering run. In parallel, runQuestion1 as a bounded internal audit; its denominator and failure cases correct the selection inherent in listing only variable families. Evidence of a capacity-to-stroke relationship must be evaluated on raw measurements and held-out objects, never by fitting an inferred capacity to the very text used to predict it. Tablet width/height/thickness is not vessel capacity or ration size.
