# Independent review of the direct Indus pattern diagnostic

Read `pattern_test.py`, `pattern_protocol.md`, and `pattern_results.json`. Independently recomputed the corpus counts and Tamil matches using pairwise equality of every pair of positions, rather than the program's first-occurrence canonical signature. No logical implementation error found in the specified local compatibility test.

Verified: 5,679 raw records; 3,677 eligible records; 3,759 line occurrences; 2,276 distinct stored lines; 133 distinct eligible Tamil word forms. Independent computation reproduced **2,065 stored-order matches and 246 singleton candidate sets**, and **2,067 reversed-order matches and 247 singleton candidate sets**. The saved 200-shuffle results have minimum/mean/maximum matches 2,064 / 2,073.59 / 2,084. The 200 shuffles were inspected as saved results, not independently rerun in this review.

The signature correctly implements injective one-sign/one-segment substitution within each line: identical sign positions must be identical segment positions, and different signs must remain different segments. Equal signatures already require equal lengths. Permitting a new mapping for every line makes this a necessary local compatibility screen under the stated whole-line/whole-word model; no global key is solved or checked.

Material interpretation limits:

- **246 singleton candidate sets are not 246 translations.** They are unique only within this small, filtered 133-word modern Tamil inventory and the line-specific mapping assumption. There is no common key, semantic check, historical bridge or word-boundary validation. Of these singleton sets, 147 are length7, 57 length8 and35 length9, illustrating inventory sparsity rather than independent reading evidence.
- **Most matches have no repeated sign to constrain them.** Of 2,276 lines, 2,185 have no repeated sign; 2,048 of the 2,065 Tamil matches are such lines. Only17 matching lines contain repeated signs. High total coverage is therefore largely the availability of equally long words with all-distinct segments, not a distinctive Tamil signal.
- Reversing each complete line is a mathematically valid alternative-order sensitivity check. It does not establish archaeological reading direction. Reporting both without selection is appropriate; individual objects may differ in direction or mirror handling. Neither orientation tests altered segmentation, multiple words or mixed writing systems.
- Eligibility means syntactically explicit nonzero transcription with intact `+` boundaries. It does not validate photographs, source completeness flags, physical-object independence or ancient glyph identification. Stored-line deduplication removes exact repetitions but not aliases, closely related copies, transcription variants or reversal duplicates. The results are line-type counts, not counts of independent artefacts.
- Tamil shuffles retain each word's length and segment multiset before deduplication, as registered. Deduplication can in principle change resulting vocabulary size; the implementation reports each size. This is an order-disruption reference, not a historical null or an outside-family control. A lack of excess cannot reject Tamil or support another language.
- All20 comparison languages are Dravidian. Coverage differs with lexicon size, length support and repeated-segment distributions; it must not become a language ranking. Filtering on nonempty Cognacy is an eligibility restriction, although the labels themselves do not enter matching.

The protocol already discloses the central model restrictions and avoids inferential p-values. Recommended wording: **“The narrow repetition-pattern screen produced no distinctive Tamil evidence against these shuffled-reference controls; it did not test a consistent decipherment.”** Do not say it tested every possible Tamil interpretation, translated the matched lines, or disproved Tamil.
