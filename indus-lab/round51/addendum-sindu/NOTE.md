# Round 51 addendum: when is Akkadian *sindû* ("Sindh wood") attested?

29 October 2026. Lookup, not a test: no fitting, no ledger factor. Round 51's files are unchanged.

## Question
Round 51 lists *sinda* / *sindu* (Witzel 1999:24, "Sindh wood sinda, si-in-da-a, si-in-du") as candidate words borrowed from the Indus region. Names cross languages untranslated, so if the word is the name *Sindhu*, the date of its attestation decides whether it is Harappan-era evidence (Mature Harappan c. 2600–1900 BCE) or a later borrowing.

## Answer: 7th century BCE, Sennacherib only

**CAD S (vol. 15, 1984), p. 284** (ISAC free PDF, `isac-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/isac-publications/cad_s.pdf`, sha256 `45e7d136…08be`; not committed, personal-use licence):

> **sindû** (or *sindaja*, *sindu*) adj.; Indian; SB, NB.
> *timmē uššī šurmēni erēni duprāni burāši* GIŠ *si-in-da-a* (var. *si-in-du*) "pillars of ebony, cypress, cedar, *duprānu*, juniper, and Indian wood" OIP 2 123:35, also 110 vii 37; cf. ibid. 106 vi 28; ibid. 106 vi 18 and 119:21 var. **(all Senn.)**. Landsberger, WO 3 261.

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Attestations of *sindû* "Indian (wood)" | 5 passages, **all in Sennacherib's building inscriptions (704–681 BCE)** |
| Periods given by CAD | SB, NB (Standard and Neo-Babylonian), i.e. 1st millennium only |
| Spellings | GIŠ *si-in-da-a*, var. *si-in-du* (exactly Witzel's two forms) |
| Related | *sindakku* (SB, meaning uncertain, "probably by-form of *sindû*"), one Late Babylonian love-lyric tablet, BM 41005 |
| 3rd or early 2nd millennium | **None in CAD** |
| Homograph to keep out | *simdu* / *sindu* "a milled cereal product", Old Akkadian to Neo-Babylonian, spelled *si-im-du*, *si-in-du*. Unrelated; a "*sindu*" in an early text is this word unless shown otherwise. |

## What it means
1. **The Mesopotamian record of *Sindhu* starts about 1,200 years after the Harappan cities.** It is not evidence of what Harappans called their land or river.
2. **It is also about two centuries older than Achaemenid *Hindush* (Darius, c. 518 BCE),** so it did not come in through Persian administration. By Sennacherib's time the river name *Sindhu* had long been in use: it is in the Rigveda, c. 1500–1200 BCE. A trade name for a timber "from Sindh" fits.
3. **The Meluhha / *sindu* puzzle dissolves.** Meluhha is the 3rd- and early 2nd-millennium name; *sindû* is 1st-millennium. By then "Meluhha" had drifted to mean Nubia (compare *šamru*, row 20 of `candidates.tsv`).
4. **The CAD gloss "Indian" follows Landsberger (WO 3 261).** The texts say only GIŠ *sindâ*, a timber listed with ebony and cedar. "From Sindh" is an inference from the sound, a strong one but still an inference.
5. **Whether *Sindhu* itself is older than Vedic, a Harappan-era name taken over by Indo-Aryan speakers, is a separate question.** The cuneiform record does not reach back far enough to answer it.

## Bearing on round 51
- No change to the verdict (NO FAMILY ATTRIBUTABLE), which rests on the pre-registered 11-word set.
- *sinda* and *sindu* are two of the eight words in the strict set (Amendment 1), which was descriptive only. This note shows that, like *šamru*, they are 1st-millennium words. They cannot be loans taken from a Harappan-era language at contact, and they are one name, not two independent words. The strict set's mild Proto-Dravidian lead (P(rank 1) 0.41) therefore rests on even less than it appeared. It is not re-run, since it was never verdict-bearing.
