# Indus sign-merging sensitivity analysis

**Status: version 1 working draft, not peer reviewed.** The analysis is frozen,
reproducible and self-contained. Plain-language write-up:
<https://igorsramos.com/blog/indus-script-sign-merging>.

Read this file, then `PAPER.md`, then `AUDIT.md`. The audit matters more than usual:
**three successive headline claims were retracted during the work**, and the reasons are
the most useful thing the project produced.

By Igor Ramos, working with three AI systems (Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini); see
`PAPER.md` §9 for who did what.

---

## What this is

Statistical claims about the Indus script are computed over a *sign inventory* — a
decision about which marks count as the same sign. Rival inventories disagree. This
project measures what happens to those statistics when you apply the documented
disagreements, and what it takes to make the answer interpretable.

**The finding, as it currently stands:**

1. A raw change in conditional entropy under sign merging is **not evidence of
   anything**. The same 67-family merge moves it from −2.9% to +4.4% depending only on
   which corpus it is applied to.
2. Calibrated against constructed references, the Indus corpus sits **between what
   context-free allographs produce and what contextually informative variants produce** —
   the documented merge families are partially context-distinguishable.
3. A corpus with **no sequential structure at all** has 57% of the real corpus's measured
   mutual information, and the same merge removes *more* from it. Any MI claim at this
   sample size has to clear that floor.

## Reproduce it

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && ./.venv/bin/pip install numpy scikit-learn matplotlib Pillow
./.venv/bin/python verify.py                              # standalone checker, exits 0
PYTHONHASHSEED=0 ./.venv/bin/python src/counterfactuals.py  # regenerates the paper's §4
```

`verify.py` imports nothing from `src/`. It recomputes the pair counts, both entropies,
mutual information by three algebraically distinct routes, the decomposition, family
disjointness and the DPI margin from the versioned inputs, and exits non-zero on any
mismatch. All inputs are vendored; no network or scratchpad access is needed.

## Things that will bite you if you resume

- **The decision set is a moving target.** `mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus` is an
  actively developed work in progress, not a published concordance. Re-cloning at a
  newer commit may silently change the 76/67 family counts. It is vendored and pinned
  for exactly this reason.
- **Wells IDs are zero-padded (`W017`); corpus codes are not (`G17`).** Naive slicing
  silently dropped 52 of 498 signs and 9 testable families for most of this project.
  Use `wells_to_g`.
- **A resampled null cannot produce a p-value below the reciprocal of its own support.**
  Two-member families have only 172–782 distinct null configurations. Reported p-values
  of 0.0002 against them were meaningless; the per-family results are withdrawn.
- **Empirical MI is mostly bias here.** Always compute the iid floor before claiming an
  MI difference means anything.
- **`H(Y|X)` is direction- and normalisation-dependent.** Reversing pairs gives −0.28%
  instead of −0.91%; dividing by `log2 V` gives **+3.51%**, the opposite sign. Mutual
  information is invariant to both.
- **24.6% of the corpus repeats an existing sign sequence.** Distinct objects, but
  report both corpora.

## Dead ends, so you don't repeat them

- **Decipherment is not available.** No bilingual, unknown target language, mean
  inscription length 4.44 signs — too short to expose inflection, so Kober-style
  analysis is structurally unavailable.
- **A "hold out post-2015 finds" design has no data.** The premise that ~850 inscriptions
  were published 2016–2022 has no support; it appears to be a misreading of ICIT's "869
  artefacts with iconography," which was a reclassification. No public corpus carries
  publication dates.
- **Wells's corpus does not exist as data** — published aggregate statistics only.
- **Re-deriving the sign list from plate images is not viable at current scan quality.**
  Only ~56% of the relevant occurrences are legible; see `FINDINGS.md`. The large CC0
  Digital Library of India scan is text-optimised and destroys every halftone
  photograph. CISID, in development, would change this.

The code and documentation are released under the **GNU GPL v3** (`LICENSE`), because the
main working corpus derives from a GPL-3.0 repository. Vendored third-party data keeps its

  `mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus`, MIT (see `data/external/mayig/LICENSE`).

**Not included**, because we can't redistribute them: the CISI plate scans (copyrighted),
the Mahadevan 1977 (M77) corpus export (not public bulk data), and the CDLI-derived

(`src/null_analysis.py`, `src/power_test.py`, `src/length_sweep.py`,
`src/extract_cdli.py`). Those scripts don't run from a fresh clone; nothing in
`PAPER.md` depends on them.
