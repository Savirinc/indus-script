The biggest keys did the most work: made for one letter, they went on to read the rest of its series. Every key has its own link: copy it from the card. About the data Every link is taken from an explicit statement in the target's notes: that a letter was read with a named key, unchanged or adapted, that a key was rebuilt from that letter's own material, or that a named key was tested on it and did not fit. Links are not inferred from resemblance. A key counts as rebuilt here when this project recovered it, from the ciphertext, from glosses or from a decipherment; keys recovered by other people (Lasry, Tomokiyo, Somogyi, and outside contributors) are scholars' keys ; keys of the period are those that survive in the archives or on DECODE. A scholar's key is placed at the date of the cipher, not of its publication, which the card gives separately. Several of George Lasry's tables for the French correspondence of the 1520s and 1530s appear as one key. Letters read straight from a contemporary decipherment, with no key rebuilt, are not shown.
