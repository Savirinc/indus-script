# Blind photograph observations — reviewer B

Scope: visual inspection of A.jpg, B.jpg and C.jpg only, in their displayed orientations. No captions, proposed readings or other annotations were consulted. These are non-expert observations from photographs, not an authentication, dating assessment or expert validation. “Incision” below means a groove that looks deliberately cut in the photograph; intention and production sequence cannot be established from these views.

## A

- A dark, roughly horizontal fragment carries several narrow linear grooves, principally in its upper half.
- In the upper-left quarter, at least two oblique lines approach a lower meeting area, making an open, downward-pointing angular form. Additional short oblique strokes appear near the upper portions. Their exact connections are uncertain: some are faint and some approach the upper broken edge. I would not confidently assign a complete branch count.
- Near the middle, a relatively long, near-vertical/slightly sloping groove descends from the upper edge toward the lower middle. A long oblique groove approaches it from the upper left. Several shorter oblique grooves appear near its upper portion. Some endpoints are cut off by the fragment's top edge.
- At upper right of center, another small group of converging/sloping grooves forms an open angular junction, again with possible short branches near the upper edge. It is smaller than the central group and not equally well preserved.
- A prominent dark, irregular discontinuity runs from the upper edge, right of center, down toward the central convergence area, then continues to the lower edge. Its variable width, jagged course and apparent separation of the surface distinguish it from the finer grooves. I treat this as a fracture rather than count it as a designed stroke. Its overlap with the central grooves makes the central lower endpoint and apparent right arm particularly uncertain.
- Several other faint surface lines could be scratches, wear or additional grooves. They should not be silently incorporated into a reconstructed figure.
- The repeated local feature is converging oblique lines with apparent short branches. The photograph does not establish three identical, complete units.

## C

- In the center are two vertically arranged, downward-pointing triangular outlines, or near-outlines. Each has an approximately transverse upper bar and two descending oblique sides. The upper triangle is wider; the lower one is narrower.

- At the far right, several grooves radiate upward/rightward from a common low-left area: a steep arm, an oblique arm and a shallow rightward arm are visible. At least some approach or terminate at the broken edge, leaving the full original form unknown.
- The right-hand fan and the central triangular pair are separated by an expanse of surface. They should not be joined into one connected shape.
- Numerous pits, worn patches and irregular scratches remain low-confidence marks. No other long fracture is as conspicuous as the main fracture in A, but local chipping complicates the central junction.

## Comparison limited to visible geometry

All three photographs contain oblique strokes and converging angular junctions. A and B also show possible short branches on longer strokes, providing a closer elementary resemblance to each other than either has to C's central pair of triangular outlines. C repeats a triangular unit twice; A suggests multiple open branched groups; B shows one large open angular group. These descriptions differ in closure, number and arrangement of units, endpoint preservation and branch certainty.

I cannot establish one shared, specific complex shape across all three from the visible evidence. The defensible commonality is elementary geometry: lines that converge, open angles and local branching. Claims of a more exact match would require choosing uncertain marks, treating a fracture as a stroke, omitting visible lines, or reconstructing missing portions. None of those choices is warranted by these photographs alone. This conclusion concerns visual correspondence only and does not establish the function or interpretation of the marks.
