# Independent photographic comparison — reviewer B, second test

I inspected only the supplied scan images `v1-page261.png` and `v2-page233.png`, including their original-resolution displays. I did not consult transcriptions, readings, captions beyond the printed image identifiers, or other reviewers' notes. This is a non-expert photographic assessment.

## Accounting for the pictures

The requested material represents four object identifiers: H-310, M-1439, M-1441 and M-1442. The paired capital/lowercase pictures under H-310, M-1441 and M-1442 must not be counted as additional independent objects. Their arrangements reverse left and right between the paired pictures. Comparison therefore has to follow the corresponding middle mark, rather than a fixed position or a presumed common lighting direction. The photographs alone do not establish the physical process used to produce each paired view.

## What is visible

- **H-310 A/a:** The middle mark has a pointed upper outline and a lower extension, but its outline and lower meeting area are uneven. In the lowercase view, I can see a small dark, approximately longitudinal/slightly oblique feature within the upper outlined area. This is a plausible candidate for the proposed interior mark. It is much shorter and less cleanly defined than the major outer grooves. In the capital view, the corresponding area is crowded with uneven dark/light patches and rough surface features; I cannot trace a comparably crisp, independently bounded interior groove there. The paired views do not give me a secure start point, end point and continuous course for an extra cut.
- **M-1439 A:** A middle pointed outline is visible, though the picture is dark and small. No separate interior stroke is clearly resolved. The interior and borders contain shading; absence of a cleanly resolved stroke is weaker evidence than a well-lit smooth interior would be.
- **M-1441 A/a:** The middle mark again has a pointed upper outline and a downward continuation. In the lowercase view especially, light and dark edges make the outer form recognizable. The interior has uneven contrast, and the area around the lower junction is irregular. I cannot securely isolate a short additional interior groove of the same sort as the H-310 candidate. The two pictures are views of the same object identifier, not two confirmations from separate objects.
- **M-1442 A/a:** The pointed upper outline and lower continuation are relatively recognizable, with substantial illumination contrast between their edges and interior. I do not see an unambiguous separate interior stroke. Some narrow internal light/dark structure could arise from the groove walls, surface texture or lighting; I would not promote that contrast into an extra line without a clearer image. Again the paired pictures provide two views of one object identifier.

## Decision at this scan resolution

**A candidate small interior mark in H-310 is visibly defensible as a provisional observation; an established extra deliberately cut stroke that distinguishes H-310 from all three comparators is not secure from these scans.** The strongest support is the small dark feature in H-310 a. Its weakness is that its endpoints and separation from roughness are poorly resolved, and the corresponding feature in H-310 A is difficult to isolate. The other pictures do not clearly display a matching independent interior groove, but their reproduction quality also limits strong absence claims.

I would record the proposed difference as **possible, requiring better surface evidence**, not as a confirmed present/absent distinction. The images do support a broadly comparable outer configuration, with variation in proportions, irregularity and preservation. No conclusion about sound, meaning or intended object follows from this assessment.
