# Round 50 — Census of Indus-sign seal impressions on inscribed cuneiform tablets (the "near-bilingual" route)

Pre-registered 2026-10-18 before any tablet or publication was examined for this purpose. Changes go in an "Amendments" section with the reason.

## Question
No object carries Indus signs and cuneiform together (round 34). The nearest thing would be an Indus-sign stamp seal **impressed on an inscribed tablet** whose text names the parties. Gulf-type and Indus-type seals were in use at Ur, Susa and in the Gulf around 2000–1700 BCE, and Mesopotamian contracts were sealed by their parties. Does any such impression exist, and if so does the seal text behave as the name of a person named on the tablet?

## Hypotheses
- **H1 (anchor exists):** at least one tablet bears an impression with legible Indus signs and names a sealer. Then the seal text is a candidate spelling of that name.
- **H1-strong (consistency):** across two or more such tablets, same sealer ⇒ same sign sequence; different sealers ⇒ different sequences. This is checkable with no sound values at all, and a violation falsifies the "seal text = owner's name" reading for these objects.
- **H0:** no impression with Indus signs on an inscribed tablet exists in the published record. Then the route is closed and documented.

## Procedure (fixed)
1. **Census sources**, in this order, all read in full for the purpose: Gadd 1932 (*Seals of Ancient Indian Style Found at Ur*, PBA 18); Hallo & Buchanan 1965 (*A "Persian Gulf" seal on an Old Babylonian mercantile agreement*); Mitchell 1986 (*Indus and Gulf type seals from Ur*); Legrain 1951 (*Ur Excavations X: Seal Cylinders*); Figulla & Martin 1953 (*UET 5*, Old Babylonian letters and documents from Ur) for sealed tablets; CDLI catalogue and ATF for Ur Old Babylonian texts with seal legends or impressions described; Amiet / de Mecquenem for Susa; Lothal and Kalibangan sealings on the Indian side; Parpola & Laursen–Steinkeller 2017 summary of Gulf seals on tablets. Anything not reachable is listed as unread.
2. **Record** every impression of a Gulf-type or Indus-type seal on an inscribed tablet: tablet number, museum number, CDLI P-number, date, text genre, names in the text with roles (seller, buyer, witness), description of the impression, whether Indus signs are present and legible, published drawing or photo reference, and the transcribed sign sequence in ICIT codes where possible.
3. **Decide** by the table alone: count of impressions with Indus signs (A), with Gulf iconography only (B), with uncertain signs (C).
4. If A ≥ 1: for each, list the candidate sealer(s) from the tablet text (Mesopotamian sealing practice: seller and witnesses seal, buyer usually does not). If A ≥ 2: apply H1-strong.

## Verdict rules
- **CLOSED** if A = 0 after all listed sources are read: the near-bilingual route does not exist in the published record. Report B and C for the record.
- **ANCHOR-CANDIDATE** if A = 1: one sequence ↔ one short list of names. Not an anchor until replicated; feeds round 35-style testing only if names accumulate.
- **ANCHOR-SET** if A ≥ 2 and H1-strong holds: a pre-registered follow-up round tests the sequences against the names with the round 47 instrument (Linear B control at matched size).
- **CONTRADICTION** if A ≥ 2 and H1-strong fails: the seal text on these objects is not the owner's name; report, and reopen the question of what Gulf seal texts record.
- **INCOMPLETE** if a listed source could not be obtained; name it and stop short of CLOSED.

## What this does not do
No sound values are proposed. No reading is attempted. Gulf-type seals without Indus signs (pure Dilmun iconography) are recorded but are outside H1. Impressions on uninscribed clay tags and bullae are recorded separately as context, not as near-bilinguals.

## Limits stated in advance
Impressions are often partial; sign legibility depends on published drawings, which are not photographs. The Ur tablets are Old Babylonian (c. 1900–1750 BCE), after the Indus cities, so any Indus-sign seal there is a Gulf product using borrowed signs (rounds 30–31), and its "name" would be a Dilmunite's, not a Harappan's. That still anchors signs to sounds if it exists.
