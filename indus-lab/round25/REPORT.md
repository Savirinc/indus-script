# Elephant-tablet comparison

11 October 2026. Follow-up to the two elephant-labelled inscription strings in round24.

## Scope

Eleven CSV records form the proposed contrast. Four, M-527–530, store 001–740–923–033–706. Seven, M-1482–1486, M-1488 and M-2042, store 740–923–033–706. These are catalogue transcriptions, not fresh readings.

Published photographs of ten of the eleven objects were inspected: CISI1 printed132–133/PDF168–169 and CISI2 printed207–209/PDF242–244. M-2042 was not located or photographically verified in this checkpoint. The original CISI2 scan was downloaded again and is preferred to the poor searchable derivative. Both versions are preserved for transparency. No AI restoration was used.

## What the photographs establish

The clearer reverse faces support the broad elephant classification in both sets. Examples include M-527 B, M-1482 B and M-1486 B bis. Visible repeated features include a trunked quadruped and, on some clearer examples, a small object below/in front of the head. Wear prevents scoring every accessory consistently across all objects. The bright lines in some publication photographs may involve highlighting; they cannot be treated as untreated surface measurements.

The text bands share conspicuous ordered forms, including a jar-like outline, a curved form, vertical strokes and a U-shaped form. Their resemblance supports comparing the group. It does not certify all strokes or the proposed extra sign.

The small-mark difference is not yet independently established across the groups. Several supposed four-sign specimens are worn, cracked or fragmentary. Faint marks near the relevant end cannot automatically be scored as either absent or additional signs. Font glyph 001 is a short stroke in the modern transcription system, not an established sound or numerical value. We do not claim that a short visible mark equals this glyph without independent transcription.

| Inspected publication grouping | Four in CISI1 | Six in CISI2; M-2042 not inspected | Publication/source grouping is a confound to investigate, not a demonstrated explanation |

The database's `complete=Y` field occurs on every record. Its separate preservation fields explicitly include chipped and partly damaged objects, consistent with the broken photographs. Therefore complete transcription and physical preservation must remain separate variables. This is not evidence that the database itself asserts every object is physically intact.

## Decision

The comparison does not yet isolate a verified ancient optional prefix. It also does not reveal a consistent animal-image, size, excavation-area or dated-context change accompanying the stored 001. Failure to establish a difference with these images is not proof that no difference existed.

Do not use this pair to infer singular/plural, a quantity, an office, a personal name or a Dravidian sound. The immediate task is a source-level transcription comparison: why do the published/digitised records segment the first four as five signs and the others as four? We need the catalogue's actual sign-by-sign edition and clearer endpoint evidence, including M-2042. The photographs and machine-readable record subset are preserved here so this question is reproducible rather than a speculative language mapping.
