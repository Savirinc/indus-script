## What the registrations/results distinction changes

- A prospective threshold is a useful discipline, but crossing it does not turn the explanatory label into a decoded meaning. Many “held” parts are controls, arbitrary prevalence thresholds, mechanical consequences of definitions or results later qualified by robustness checks.
- Several sets declare an untestable part a failure. The held/failed tally therefore mixes contradicted predictions, underpowered tests, unsupported extrapolations and unavailable cases. It is not a score for percent deciphered.
- Examples of registered deviations are disclosed: 1,000 rather than 10,000 permutations, revised pair counting, and bugs corrected before recording. These must accompany any replication plan rather than silently inheriting a claimed design.
- Numerous null claims are operationalised as p ≥ .05 (e.g. independence or no direction effect). These are failures to detect an effect, not equivalence evidence. The text sometimes acknowledges limited power; it should remain explicit in our summaries.
- Thousands of evolving tests cannot be read as independent confirmatory discoveries merely because each next batch was registered. This interval mostly uses per-test .05 without a single stated experiment-wide multiplicity budget. Dependence, post-selection, reuse of A/B and repeated artefacts must be handled in a fresh experiment.

## Open questions and concrete implications for our research

1. **Photo-grounded units and orientation.** Can we identify physical objects, their faces, impressions/mould relatives and source-specific mirrors before analysis? Sets 24 and 64–66 already show why catalogue order is insufficient. Preserve line boundaries, face boundaries and uncertainty; do not infer canonical direction solely because it strengthens a preferred grammar.
2. **Affix decomposition.** Is a stroke consistently real across independent photographed 220/231 objects, rather than font design, damage or editorial distinctions? If so, what observable distinguishes a composed sequence from a modified class member under a matched, held-out test? Set 25 already tested a basic additive model and found no support.
3. **Quantity versus category.** Different stroke counts on otherwise similar short texts fit both a quantity hypothesis and conventional class labels. The many changes from “counts” to “idioms” and back show that distributional association alone does not settle semantics. Independent dimensions or capacities, secure find context, or another externally measured relation are needed; three selected vessels would provide a small consistency audit, not a decipherment.
4. **Pictures as reference.** What exactly is paired: a sign, full text, object family, reverse picture, institution or scene? Reverse substitutions are better-defined evidence than importing a drawing into the obverse transcription. Text/picture predictability must survive object-family deduplication and broad versus fine motif-label checks.
5. **Meaning labels as hypotheses.** Can an alternative account—office, commodity, owner, workshop or emblem—make different predictions on data not already used? If not, keep the result structural. Terms such as name, receipt, suffix, person class and count token should be displayed as operational labels or hypotheses, never translations.
6. **A fresh test must add information.** Our symbol census, end-position tests, modifier clustering, sign decomposition and known-language control idea substantially repeat routes present here. The useful next deliverable is a provenance-backed exclusion/inclusion and artefact-family ledger, or a genuinely new external observation with a preregistered falsification criterion; no further unconstrained loop is justified by this prose audit alone.
