## Section 13: what each constraint actually means

| Stated constraint | What it means for us | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| No forced sound value | Several different sound assignments can fit observed patterns; an independent sound–sign link is missing | No possible future inference can identify sounds |
| Language unidentified | Structural compatibility and plausible dictionary matches have not discriminated candidates against adequate controls | Sanskrit, Tamil, Dravidian or another family has been proved correct or universally excluded |
| Internal testing has run out | This team's tested representations and successive hypothesis queues reached diminishing returns and stopping criteria | A mathematical proof that every internal method is exhausted; the notes themselves record later work after earlier stopping claims |
| Need new material | New independent observations can discriminate explanations that the present encoding cannot | Only a newly excavated object can help; better photographs, reliable face matching or an independent transcription can correct measurements on existing objects |
| Full ICIT already used | Their larger export was already held; the clean analysis sample was smaller because of filtering | Their export contains every known Indus object, every photograph, or all archaeological context |
| Copper sign–image equations | Some published groupings link alternate reverse representations to a shared inscription | A sign occurring in running text has been translated as an animal word or given a pronunciation |
| Name/office/lineage unresolved | Short recurring compositions might bundle several functions | Calling them 'names' in code has demonstrated personal-name semantics |

The recorded export has 5,680 records; the loader retained 3,681 clean records and excluded 1,999 damaged ones. The final note estimates about 4,000 unused legible signs in those damaged records. These are the author's reported counts, not a fresh reproduction here. A damaged record must retain its missing spans: deleting gaps could create sign adjacencies that never existed. Adding its legible segments would expand the usable sample, not create a bilingual or an independent archaeological universe.

Digitised CISI 2/3 can add photos, face information, editorial readings and previously unrepresented observations. Object-level overlap must be checked: another transcription of an already included object is measurement corroboration, not a new independent inscription.

## A correction hidden behind the strong copper wording

The main webpage calls the seven sign–image relationships its firmest meanings. NOTES.md is more specific:

- Pass 46, lines 1164–1170, records that 341/rhinoceros, 749 or 778/goat and 753/hare do not occur in the ICIT inscription labels associated with their own pictures. It withdraws them **as text anchors**.
- Pass 47, lines 1177–1179, retains their narrower role: most linked groups pair an image reverse with a single-sign reverse over the same inscription.

These statements concern different roles of a mark. A sign-like design can replace a reverse picture without proving that it is a word for that animal inside normal text. It might stand for an institution, deity, emblem or another relation. The physical pairing constrains explanations; it does not choose one by itself.

Our previous plan to verify these pairs remains useful, but its deliverable is a measurement and provenance check. We must not announce a translated word after merely confirming the pairing. The separate copper-evidence.md records what was actually found in the catalogue images this round.

## Original source check completed this round

We obtained Parpola 1994 and visually inspected Fig. 7.14 on printed pages 111–112 and its accompanying caption. The figure explicitly reconstructs prototype designs by comparing surviving examples; it does not claim to show every tablet in its present condition. The seven motif–script connections are A2/C4, B1/C1, B5/A11, B7/C2, B9/C5, B10/A7 and B19/C6. The exceptional B6/B12 connection links two pictorial reverses and is excluded. This resolves the seven-versus-ten counting question raised in round 16; mirrored subgroups further expand the derivative output rows. The count itself is not evidence against the published typology.

We also inspected catalogue photographs of M-534, M-593 and M-1547. M-534 has a clear animal reverse and sign band. M-593 shares much of the visible ordered band, but its reverse is too faint in this scan to independently reconstruct the proposed sign. M-1547 also remains difficult to read. Thus the published B7/C2 relationship is now checked against the original figure, but the available photographs do not yet independently certify every stroke of both inscriptions and the alternate reverse. We retain a partial visual match with unresolved details, not a photographically proved translation. A separate visual reviewer, not shown the proposed meanings or source typology for this comparison, independently reported the same coarse resemblance and inability to certify full equality. This was a qualitative check, not a formal inter-annotator accuracy study.

## Other limitations exposed by the full records

1. **Structural vocabulary is interpretive.** Terms such as name, head, suffix, receipt and person are convenient hypotheses. Preserve neutral sign positions and sequences alongside them.
2. **More copies can inflate apparent evidence.** Seal impressions, moulded copies, multiple faces and partial transcriptions can leak across splits. Group physical objects and production families before counting independence.
3. **Direction and numbering change between sources.** A global reversal rule is unsafe; the notes contain several corrections. Preserve original orientation, export convention and any transformation per record. Crosswalks can merge distinctions and make a replication uninformative.
4. **A method can recognize an answer without discovering it.** A supplied correct Linear B key passes one character-model control; free fitting still selects the wrong language. Positive controls must match the intended task and freedom.
5. **Known-language comparisons can reward genre.** Japanese decoys overturn an apparent South Asian preference after genre matching. This tests the comparison, not a Japanese origin.
6. **Significance is not accuracy or translation.** Later picture-match results can exceed a shuffle baseline while only about 5% of held-out match observations are correct. Their coverage measure is not a percentage deciphered.
7. **Repeated testing does not create untouched evidence.** Registering the next test can document a decision but cannot undo prior exposure to the corpus. New random splits of familiar records are not fully prospective validation.

The notes also already test component-based signs: a particular additive 231=220+1 contextual hypothesis failed in set 25. That does not disprove every compositional sign system, but we cannot present 'split symbols into parts' as an unexplored idea.
