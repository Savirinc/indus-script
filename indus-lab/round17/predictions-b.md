## Specific failure constraints and self-corrections

1. **400 and the receipt narrative:** set83 results (line 4076) report no advantage for tablet bodies matching seal bodies, and no clear same-site linkage. The defensible retained observation is a context/object association, not a receipt or ownership interpretation. Later bare400 has different predecessor distributions (set109, result starts5198), so its uses cannot all be merged.
2. **“No difference” is repeatedly treated as “same”:** CG controls, free-variant tests, regional comparisons, absence of agreement, and temporal stability use p>=.05 as a holding rule. That is not an equivalence test and cannot establish invariance. Set144 (result6494) has eight failed hypotheses but concludes a stable ending slot; chronological uncertainty and low power remain.
3. **Replications can be degenerate or overlapping:** set100 (4818) notes M77 merges many ICIT types; set101 (4863) explicitly notes no817 examples and merged705/706. Set118 (5589) admits CE12 repeats CE2 on the same data. F frequently contains A/B source material; “fuller” is not synonymous with held out. Each result needs an object-level overlap audit.
4. **Field changes reverse readings:** sets94–97 (4556,4638,4682) distinguish copper labels from seal “names”; set105 (5031) corrects the apparent Mohenjo-daro closer excess when the denominator becomes seals only. Set133 (6074) finds the520-material link mostly reflects copper labels. These are direct warnings against pooled site/material/function claims.
5. **Timing and appearance:** period contrasts in sets84,130–133,144,146,153,162–165 use inherited level mappings. Our project's previously discovered date/type provenance problems must be addressed before adopting any temporal claim. Set130 (5983) explicitly infers the meaning of size field31 rather than documenting it.
6. **Shape is not sound or identity:** set137 (6206) reports no overall shape/context correlation and unsuccessful crude compound recovery; normalized font drawings are explicitly not artefacts. Catalogue-neighbour patterns can partly reflect catalogue construction. This supports treating our decomposition as a candidate-generating step only.
7. **CISI agreement is not fully independent:** set139 (6289) reports high agreement but concedes the M77-to-ICIT map was learned on a corpus including those objects. Rare-sign disagreements and merged allographs matter. Its limited M1–M184 sample cannot settle all-site transcription questions.
8. **Layout evidence is promising but limited:** set142 (6419) reports line boundaries seldom splitting selected bound pairs, yet acknowledges earlier versions of the test and few junctions. Its stronger language—word boundaries/syntax rather than layout—is an interpretation. Test code must distinguish separate faces, physical rows, and catalogue segmentation.
9. **Capture-recapture does not identify people:** sets147/152 initially promote a personal-name reading. Sets157 (6891) and158 (6931) explicitly qualify it: count texts are also open; Ur III person names are closed while whole legends are open. “Large population” estimates additionally depend on catchability, geography, time, and text deduplication. The safe target is compositional diversity.
10. **Packed office+name model withdrawn:** set159 (6997) initially promotes closed opener+open core; sets161/162 (7081 onwards) show this mostly compares a single sign with a longer string. Motif support weakens and does not generalize between cities.
11. **Convergence withdrawn:** set164 (7170) describes movement toward Harappan openings; set165 (7226) corrects this to a local shift toward frequent openings, without general convergence in signs, heads, or notation.
12. **Local counts are not proof of local names:** set150 (6709) interprets city-dependent numeral values as naming customs. Set168 (7311) withdraws that interpretation after comparison with real Linear B counts.
13. **The current language comparisons do not identify a language:** sets171–175 (7432,7465,7508,7557,7604) successively change the Tamil comparator and its suffix handling, then add Prakrit and Tamil-Brahmi donors. Rankings are sensitive to the comparator. Different units (Indus sign, akshara, syllable, lemma) and millennia of separation remain. “Sumerian-type excluded” is stronger than these particular sampled name/order comparisons justify.
14. **Role/meaning metrics are not decipherment:** sets176/177 (7653,7686) expand R by assigning token roles from parsed position even when the type-level generalization test fails. Set178 (7715) defines M+ using depiction classes and ending distributions;740's high base rate is an admitted limitation. These are classification coverages, not validated meanings for that proportion of tokens.
15. **No phonetic breakthrough here:** set179 (7748) reports no proposed key passing its A/B gate and P remains0. Set180 (7779) reports that glossary-first lookup chooses unsuitable rare words, emptying the language comparison. Set181's subsequent method is only introduced before my assigned range ends; I make no claim about its outcome.

## Implementation and reporting issues to inspect next, not verified bugs

- **Reading-order convention is high priority.** Set170 results (7389) report that left-to-right strings match right-to-left strings as stored46 times versus2 reversed, and infer ICIT already stores reading order. Our original positional analysis reversed R/L text. This is a concrete reason to check source documentation and photograph-aligned examples before reusing beginning/end conclusions. Do not infer the convention merely from this unrerun count, but do not ignore the conflict.
- **One sign inconsistency in the prose:** CE6's registration asks for a negative closer-share/740-share correlation; set118's result prints Spearman0.79 while claiming replacement and counting the test as held. Inspect the actual script/result to determine whether a minus sign was lost, the statistic differs, or the criterion was applied incorrectly.
- **Ties and ill-posed tests remain in the tally:** set176 says LP8 holds on a tie although the registration says a model beats baseline. Set155 calls DT3 an artefact but counts it held; set167 says AD9's home rate is zero by construction. Set163's single-lemma shuffle is also degenerate. Raw held/failed totals must not be interpreted as posterior support or independent successful discoveries.
- **Predictive scores need a conditional-likelihood audit.** Position/end-distance models know the line's position and often its length; genre-specific models may use a line's final sign. These can be valid reconstruction/conditional models, but are not automatically ordinary left-to-right generative likelihoods. Check normalized probabilities, unknown handling, leakage through feature construction, and comparison denominators. Do not call the reported scores verified benchmarks yet.
- **Repeated fixed test use creates researcher adaptation.** Sets122–126 and176–177 repeatedly examine the same random split while proposing new components. Training-only weight fitting helps, but does not restore an untouched final test set after sequential model design.
- **Automatic raw motif absence is unsafe.** Sets94/98/151 collapse literalNone and missing labels into no picture, and use exclusive categories. Our M-482/Mult problem directly applies: absence of a named category is not absence of that motif. New photographic multi-label annotations are needed.
- **Derived upstream readings and classes are not raw evidence.** Set134 uses a text-derived classifier, and135 uses stored romanized readings/glosses of unspecified provenance. Their consistency tests are tests of those annotations; they cannot supply independent decipherment targets.
- **Heuristic sound tests are evidence, not universal gates.** The minimal-pair similarity test works on the chosen Linear B control but need not hold for every language, logographic convention, rebus, or name system. Failure can reflect coverage, noisy mapping, small samples, or an unsuitable statistic. Conversely a single pass would not validate a key.

## Open questions suitable for the next concrete audit

1. Which proposed invariants survive an object/face-aware, duplicate-aware corpus with photo-confirmed reading direction and sign identity?
2. Which failed predictions are genuinely contradicted, and which are untestable, underpowered, tautological, threshold near-misses, or based on non-equivalence tests? Track these separately.
3. Can repeated physical layouts and verified complementary faces constrain segmentation without assuming that an endpoint defines a name?
4. Does any sign/context association generalize across independently annotated objects after stratifying type, material, site, chronology, and copy families?
5. Can a proposed semantic or phonetic key beat strong sequence-only and random-key baselines on a genuinely untouched object set, with the scoring method calibrated on multiple writing systems?
6. Can original publication pages resolve the numeral-code and reading-direction conventions that most downstream models assume?

The concrete stopping point in this portion of the ledger is **structured but unanchored interpretation**: many recurring positional/distributional observations and useful failed routes, but no independently validated sound values or uniquely identified language. Preserve that distinction when integrating these experiments into our project.
