# Round 17 — copper-tablet photograph audit

## Result and scope

The B7/C2 hare–sign comparison is a real, inspectable catalogue lead. We now have the original published prototype figure and photographs of both faces of several candidate tablets. The photographs support repeated ordered graphic material on M-534 A and M-593 A. They do not yet independently establish every sign match or the complete C2 reverse reconstruction. No word, sound, deity, language, or animal-name reading is established.

This review inspected the pages listed below, not the complete books. Photographs are evidence of the objects; the drawings in Parpola’s grouping diagram are scholarly reconstructions. White markings on some photographs may represent publication-era tracing/highlighting; their precise treatment was not established here. They must not automatically be treated as untouched surface photographs.

## Seven links versus ten output rows: source issue resolved

Parpola’s Fig. 7.14 caption explicitly distinguishes seven image–inscription connections from an exceptional image–image linkage, B12/B6, which uses the reverse inscription. Thus the website’s seven motif–script relationships and its program’s ten rows are not automatically contradictory. Mirrored variants and the exceptional B12/B6 row explain the bookkeeping difference. Round16’s unresolved count question is superseded by this direct source check.

The seven prototype links are A2/C4, B1/C1, B5/A11, B7/C2, B9/C5, B10/A7, and B19/C6. These are group links, not seven photographed object pairs independently verified in this audit.

The caption says the figure reconstructs prototypes by comparing examples; uncertain reconstruction is shown with broken lines and poorly preserved/unclassifiable examples are excluded. Thick frames identify linked inscriptions. B7 is assigned 14 examples; C2, two. Those numbers are the author’s group inventory, not counts independently recomputed here. The subsequent interpretation as divinities is the author’s semantic hypothesis, not a photographic observation.

## What is now testable

A blinded comparison can ask whether independently transcribed visible A-face features agree across M534, M535, M593 and M1547, recording uncertain positions instead of filling them from Parpola’s prototype. The B faces must be annotated separately before labels are revealed. Agreement in a shared A string paired with different B motifs would support a controlled association. It would still not establish that the motifs have the same linguistic meaning: ownership, administrative class, ritual grouping and other functions remain possible.

Next decisive acquisition is a clearer C2 reverse image, preferably original photography or a separately published early excavation plate for M593/M1547, together with catalogue membership/context. More model fitting to the reconstructed font string does not resolve missing strokes.

This is qualitative corroboration, not proof of group membership or a measured inter-annotator agreement rate.
