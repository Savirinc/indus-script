# Round 59: Traded-goods words in Brahmi-key readings. **VOID** (positive control failed; Indus results reported as description only)

Pre-registered in `PROTOCOL.md` (commit ec1b495, lexicons frozen with it). `run59.py` locked in 49b9af0. One run.

## Verdict: VOID
The Linear B control did not reach the bar. The true Linear B key found its trade words (gold, ivory, blue glass, cumin, axes, amphorae…) more often than shuffled keys, but not clearly enough: T 22 against a shuffle mean of 14.2 (sd 3.7), z 2.11, percentile 0.968. The bar was z ≥ 3 and percentile ≥ 0.99.

Only 10 of the 26 Linear B trade words have skeletons of three or more consonants. Most Greek trade words are short (*ka-ko*, *e-ra-wo*, *me-ri*), so the instrument has too little to work with. **At Indus density, this method cannot reliably detect a small vocabulary of trade words even in a script where they are known to be present.** Under rule 6, nothing below is scored. Ledger: K7 unchanged, no factor.

## The clearest lesson: the same letters "mean" whatever you look for
The commonest matches show why readings like these persuade people and still mean nothing.

|---|---:|---|---|
| t-m-n | 65 | *tamomaṇi*, "a kind of gem" | *timman-*, "a monkey" |
| n-m-n | 106 | *unmaṇi*, "a gem lying on the surface" | |

| v-s-m | 70 | | *vēẓam*, "elephant" |
| s-r-n | 38 | | *saṛan-*, "ox, bull" |

The same 65 seal texts give a gem in Sanskrit and a monkey in Proto-Dravidian. A reader who looks for gems finds gems, and a reader who looks for monkeys finds monkeys. The shuffled keys find just as many. This is the "easy to vary" pattern in Deutsch's sense, shown in numbers.

## What this does not say
It does not show that the seals have nothing to do with trade. It shows that this key, read this way, cannot find trade words in a way that can be told apart from chance. It also shows that this instrument cannot reliably find them even in Linear B.
