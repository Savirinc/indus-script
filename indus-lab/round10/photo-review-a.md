## Reference morphology

The arms have similar lengths and are stacked at roughly regular intervals. There is no opposite vertical stem enclosing the spaces. A reflected example could put the stem on the right and arms to the left; this would preserve the basic structure.

## Side assignment and limitations

All five relevant candidate groups occur in photographs explicitly labeled **A**. That is a direct photographic face link. It is not a verification that a database's abstract side number, sign identifier, or entire transcription corresponds to that photographed A face. Such a crosswalk requires separate record evidence. I do not claim a corresponding reference comb on any B photograph in this set.

Reflection is permitted in the comparison, but no image was silently reoriented: the M-478 candidate and H-174's right-end candidate have the reverse left/right arrangement from the supplied font. Reflection preserves the comb structure without determining a reading direction.

**M-478 requires an explicit transcription warning.** The supplied task reports a raw record containing only four tokens, while the A photograph visibly contains a substantially longer row with numerous distinct-looking forms or groups. The photograph cannot tell whether that record is abbreviated, partial, differently segmented, mismatched, or otherwise incomplete. Four tokens must not be treated as a verified complete transcription or aligned to all the visible forms. Only the local comb-like candidate and its photographed A-face location are supported here.

**Overall:** A-face placement is confident for the photographed candidates. The coarse comb morphology is clearest in H-172, H-174, and M-478. M-470 and M-482 support a more qualified resemblance. These images do not independently verify that every candidate is the exact six-arm font glyph, nor do they verify complete transcriptions.
